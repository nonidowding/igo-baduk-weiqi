# Basics/Capture Go Level 2
## New Rules
To take capture go to the next level and make it more difficult, let's change the objective. All of the previous rules are still in effect except now you must capture at least 3 stones in total to win. This means first capture does not necessarily win. If you only capture 1 or 2 stones, play continues!

I suggest playing with this rule on a 9x9 board and having **three games** with a goal of capturing 3 stones. Then, you can move up to playing capture go with a 5 stone limit.

### Place Captures in the Lids

It's traditional to keep your captures in the lid of the bowl, visible, next to the board, so that you can keep track. We didn't need to do this before because one capture ended the game.

<image of captures in the lid>

### Capturing Larger Groups

It's possible to play inside a large group for a capture as long as it's the last space. If its your turn, you take the enemy stones off first, and your stone gets to survive.

## Getting Trapped: Don't Save Every Stone!

In the image below, you can see that the black stone is in a dangerous spot.

<image>

White can play atari here to attack it. We say "atari" when a stone or group of stones has only one free space left to escape into. The piece in "atari" is just one move away from capture and needs urgent help.

<image>

The Black piece can run to the edge. However, playing on the edge doesn't grow the free spaces around the group quickly enough. You can see that even when black adds stones to escape, they get captured anyway.

<gif>
  
In this case, black has lost three stones and lost the game. However, if black simply doesn't play here anymore, black will only lose one piece. A comeback is still possible! We call this a "sacrifice".

## What To Do when the Board Starts to Fill Up?

Since we've upped the difficulty, a few games will start getting to this point:

<image of a mostly full board>

The rules still apply, but the space to play in is getting tight. Just keep going until three stones are captured!

An interesting thing starts to happen in these types of games. The player who controlled more of the board during the early part of the game tends to win. Why is that? Let's look at an example.

<gif of the same board, the winning player filling in their area, but the other player runs out of space first>

As long as the player with more space doesn't make an error, they will have more safe spaces to fill in. If the other tries to invade, they will usually get captured first. With nowhere left to play, they can only accept their fate.
